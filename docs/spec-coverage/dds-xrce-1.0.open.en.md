## §10.4 Federation of agents

**Status:** `partial` — with `upstream:` the agent is a client of another agent: per routed client a session there with the client's ClientKey, session_id and properties; CREATE, DELETE, WRITE_DATA, READ_DATA and GET_INFO go there unchanged on their stream, STATUS, INFO and DATA come back on theirs; the agent itself creates no DDS entity for the client. The limits of the upstream agent apply to the objects of a routed client; of this agent's `limits` only `max_clients`. The answers of the upstream agent on a reliable stream go to the client as far as its sender window on that stream has room (16 messages, §8.4.14.1); this agent takes the others not out of the link and does not acknowledge them, so the sender window of the upstream agent fills and it takes no more samples for the client out of its DataReaders. The client's ACKNACK hands over what waited and acknowledges it to the upstream agent at once. While the link holds answers no keepalive goes; the HEARTBEATs of the upstream agent for the held messages keep the link alive. Measured with 100 samples and a client that does not acknowledge: the client gets 16 DATA, this agent holds no DATA beyond the window, the upstream agent has taken at most 32 samples out of its queue and holds no DATA beyond its window; after the client's ACKNACKs all 100 arrive once and in order. The access rules of the upstream agent decide its operations (§7.4); an `access_rules` entry of this agent that covers a routed client stops the start. A FRAGMENT run goes out whole or not at all; what the sender window has no room for follows after the upstream agent's ACKNACKs, also for more than 16 FRAGMENTs. A request on a reliable stream stays with the agent until the upstream agent acknowledged it (WRITE_DATA, READ_DATA) or answered it (CREATE, DELETE, GET_INFO). While the upstream agent cannot be reached the requests wait (store and forward); a full queue, and a request the MTU of the link cannot carry, are answered `STATUS_ERR_RESOURCES`, and so is a request on a best-effort stream or on STREAMID_NONE that is larger than one message of the link (65 507 octets over UDP and IPv4, 65 527 over UDP and IPv6, 65 535 over TCP): it cannot be split into FRAGMENTs and does not hold up the requests behind it. An `upstream.mtu` above one message of the link is refused by the configuration. A lost session is opened again with the other of two session_ids (the client's and the next one, never the one of the session that was open last; §7.8.2.1 replaces one still held), then objects and running reads are created again and the requests neither acknowledged nor answered are sent again in their order: none is lost, a request whose acknowledgement was lost with the link may be carried out twice. The first session of a link replaces any session the upstream agent still holds for the client (from an earlier run of this agent, or one whose DELETE was lost): first a CREATE_CLIENT with the other session_id, after its answer one with the client's. A RESET of the client (§8.3.5.13) ends its reads at the upstream agent, each with a READ_DATA with `max_samples` 0 on its stream (§8.3.5.9.2); a DATA of them still on its way does not reach the client, and a new session does not create the reads again. A link that hears nothing for `link_timeout_ms` is opened again with a new transport. A link over `tcp://` is connected, written and read by threads of its own, never under the lock of the session engine: an upstream agent that drops the connection attempt or stops reading holds up no other client. What it does not read waits in the link up to 1 MiB beyond the socket's send buffer, then in the queue. A connected link that is given up writes what it was handed (the DELETE of a session that ended) and ends the connection within 1 s. Measured through a proxy that stops reading after the link is up, while the routed client writes 1000 best-effort samples of 60 000 octets: a client of the same agent that is not routed gets every answer within 3 s, and the silent link is opened again on a new TCP connection. Over UDP: clients of two outer agents exchange a sample through the inner agent, also across two chained agents; the outer agents cannot start a DDS runtime, and such an agent without `upstream:` refuses the participant. Requests sent before the inner agent starts (six CREATEs, a READ_DATA, a WRITE_DATA) are answered once each, in their order, after it starts, and the sample written before arrives. A new outer agent takes over the client of one that stopped, whose session the inner agent still holds; the client opens the same session again, its CREATEs are answered, and its sample reaches a reader at the inner agent. Against the eProsima Micro-XRCE-DDS Agent 3.0.1 as upstream agent: two clients of the agent create their objects there and exchange a sample (job `vendor-live-xrce`). Green on the Linux test host; the eProsima test with the agent of its superbuild and with the agent built as in the job against Fast DDS 3.6.1, three times each; with the first session of two CREATE_CLIENTs green again three times against the agent of its superbuild (the agent built as in the job against Fast DDS 3.6.1 not again). The RESET of a routed client, the link over `tcp://` and the holding of the answers while the client's sender window is full are not checked against the eProsima agent; the eProsima test did not run again after the holding was built. Open: the upstream link over `tls://` and `dtls://` (the listeners have them; `upstream.locator` refuses schemes other than `udp`, `tcp` with an error).
